ODSP Onboarding Agent
User research — personas, pain points, and what the agent needs to solve
3 interviews · 1 PM, 2 Software Engineers · April 2026
Who we spoke to
User Personas
Three new hires across different roles and backgrounds — all within their first three months on ODSP.
Product Manager
Reethik
External hire · ~6 weeks in · News Posts team
Reethik joined from outside Microsoft and was quickly asked to fill in for a colleague. He had no context on what the team was building, who the customers were, or how decisions were made. He uses Copilot through Bing and Teams for searching internal links.
"A bunch of random things were thrown at me. They're still being thrown at me."
Software Engineer
Antonio
External hire · ~2 months in · Multi-tiering team
Antonio has strong engineering experience but no Microsoft background. He uses Copilot to search for docs and wikis, but frequently hits dead ends — wrong access groups, outdated pages, or guidance that points to the right topic but the wrong version of it.
"Copilot pointed me to the right page — but not to the page that tells you how to actually get access to it."
Software Engineer

Internal transfer · ~3 months in · OD4 / Nucleus team
Mary came from the Copilot Hub team inside Microsoft. She knew the company but not ODSP, and assumed more would carry over than it did. She prefers accuracy over breadth and will stop using a tool the moment it gives her stale or made-up information.
"I'd rather be pointed to the right person than get a confident answer that turns out to be wrong."
What we heard
Pain Points
Seven clear friction areas that came up across all three interviews. These are the moments where new hires slow down, get stuck, or have to rely on someone else to unblock them.
1
The onboarding checklist is hard to follow
The new hire checklist lives in a document, but the steps reference separate email instructions, wiki links, and access requests spread across different places. New hires have to jump between multiple sources to complete what should be a single, guided flow — and it's easy to miss steps or do them in the wrong order.
"My manager also just missed a couple of things — he had me for 10 different things at once."
2
No visibility into what the team — or adjacent teams — are working on
New hires don't have a clear view of their team's current priorities or how those connect to what neighboring teams are doing. This makes it hard to understand the full picture, know who to collaborate with, or see how their own work fits into broader org goals.
"I didn't know what my team was doing — let alone what other teams were building."
3
Finding the right documents and dashboards means asking someone
There's no reliable way to find the relevant specs, wikis, or dashboards for a given project without reaching out to a teammate. Links are shared informally in meetings or chat threads, and searching for them often surfaces outdated or unrelated results.
"I piggybacked on someone else asking in the chat for the link — there was no other way to get it."
4
No clear picture of our customers, metrics, or how decisions get made
New hires — especially PMs — need to understand who uses ODSP, what they care about, and what data drives product decisions. This context isn't surfaced during onboarding, so people either make decisions without it or spend weeks piecing it together from meetings and documents they stumble across.
"I found the FY2026 vision document six weeks in — by accident. I would have loved to have it on day one."
5
Team language and acronyms aren't explained anywhere useful
The formal glossary covers official terms but misses the shorthand that teammates actually use in meetings and docs. Terms like "quit nucleus," "one-up view," or "rehydrating a blob" are used as if everyone knows them — but for a new hire, they're invisible barriers to following what's going on. Searching the glossary every time breaks focus and still often doesn't help.
"I feel shy to even ask — is this something everyone already knows and I just missed it?"
6
Operating procedures like ERs and PRs are unclear — and so is how to participate in them
New hires don't have a clear understanding of recurring team rituals like exception reviews, pull request processes, and bug bashes — or what they need to set up to participate properly. Things like being on the right product flight, knowing how to enable debug mode, or understanding what's expected of them in a review aren't explained anywhere ahead of time.
"Everyone was on the dogfood flight and I wasn't. I was seeing a completely different UI and had no idea why."
7
No map of the org — who the teams are and what they own
ODSP is large, and new hires have no structured way to understand which teams exist, what each one is responsible for, and how they relate to each other. Building this mental model currently takes months of passive observation in meetings.
"I want to know what the other teams are doing — siloing into just my team is a problem."
What the agent needs to solve
User Stories
Each story maps directly to a pain point above. These are the things new hires need to be able to do — on their own, without asking a colleague.
Any new hire
When I start at ODSP, I want a single guided checklist — with all the steps, links, and access requests in one place — so I can complete onboarding on my own without cross-referencing emails, docs, and wikis.
Any new hire
I want to see what my team is currently prioritizing and how that connects to what adjacent teams are working on — so I understand where my work fits and who I need to be aware of.
Any new hire
When I'm working on a project, I want to find the relevant specs, wikis, and dashboards without having to ask a teammate — so I can get up to speed quickly and independently.
New hire PM
I want to understand who our customers are, what metrics matter for my feature area, and how product decisions are typically made — so I can contribute meaningfully from the start instead of waiting months to build that context.
Any new hire
When I hear a term or acronym I don't recognize — whether it's in a doc, a meeting, or a code review — I want an instant explanation without having to open the glossary and search — so I can stay in flow and keep up with what's happening around me.
Any new hire
Before my first ER, PR review, or bug bash, I want to know what's expected of me and how to set up everything I need — the right flight, debug mode, and any other prerequisites — so I can participate fully instead of realizing mid-meeting that I'm not set up correctly.
Any new hire
I want a clear overview of all the teams in ODSP, what each one owns, and how they interact — so I can build a mental model of the org without having to wait for it to emerge slowly through months of meetings.
