Research Findings
Key pain points
Recurring friction across all 3 participants, regardless of role or background
1
Wrong access, wrong path "Request ODSP All" — but that's almost never the right group. The real sub-group, approver, and wait time are buried or wrong.
All 3 participants
2
Stale or contradictory docs Onboarding wikis with 404s, videos from years ago with outdated info, ramp docs that mention deprecation with no "use this instead."
Antonio, Mary
3
Flights & rings assumed as known New hires end up in meetings seeing a different UI from everyone else. Instructions assume you're already on the flight.
PM, Mary
4
Acronyms + colloquialisms not in glossary "Quit nucleus" = quit OneDrive.Sync in Task Manager. "One-up view." "Rehydrate the blob." Formal glossary doesn't cover these.
All 3 participants
5
No map of the org or what teams own No structured way to understand what other ODSP teams are building or how they connect to your work.
All 3 participants
6
Dashboards are tribal knowledge Links shared ad hoc in meeting invites or chat. No curated list. Even when found, meaningless without team context first.
PM, Mary
Research Findings
The ODSP homepage pattern
Useful in Week 1 — then forgotten. Not a recurring resource.
Week 1
Visited every 1–2 days for checklist, bio post, doc links
Week 2–3
Maybe once a week. Visiting specific links shared by others.
Month 2+
Not visited. News posts seen via direct links, not homepage.
Key insight: The homepage is a Week 1 tool. After initial tasks are done, there is no trigger to return — even when new questions come up. The org vision doc was discovered 6 weeks in, by accident. Had it been surfaced in Week 1, it would have been "even better."
Agent Design
Scenarios this agent would solve
Real situations from interviews — trigger → what the agent does
1
"What access do I request?"Day 1–3. Onboarding doc says "ODSP All" — that's wrong for most teams.
Asks your team → returns the exact security group, right approver, expected wait time. Shareable checklist.
2
"Everyone's on dogfood, I'm not."In a meeting, seeing a different UI. No idea why.
Explains flights/rings in plain language. Step-by-step to get on the right flight. Offered proactively in Week 1.
3
"What does [term] mean?""Quit nucleus." "One-up view." "Rehydrate the blob." Not in the glossary.
Plain-language definition. If unknown, routes to the right alias. Lets anyone submit new terms back.
4
"Is this doc still current?"Wiki last updated 2 years ago. Are ramps still the right way? Is V1 or V2?
Cross-references against newer sources. Flags if stale. Points to fresher doc or a human alias — never guesses.
5
"I don't know what I don't know."Day 1. No idea what to even ask.
Detects role + team from profile. Walks through top 3–5 things most people on your team needed in Week 1.
Agent Design
What makes them use it — or abandon it
Direct from participant feedback on what they'd need to give it a real shot

✓ Will use it if...

✓Knows my role, team, title — responses feel personalized, not generic
✓Has a clear onboarding flow explaining what it knows and can do
✓Gives the doc link AND the access path — not just "here's a doc"
✓Points to a human alias when it doesn't know — rather than making something up
✓Shows social proof — "X new hires found this useful"

✗ Will abandon it if...

✗Gives the same answer to a PM, SWE, or designer — feels like AI slop
✗Returns stale information presented confidently as current
✗Hallucinated answers when it doesn't actually know
✗Over-contextualizes from previous chats when asking something unrelated
✗Becomes a replacement for team connection — "just ask Claude"
Recommendations
Top 5 capabilities to build first
Prioritized by frequency of mention and severity of pain across all 3 interviews
1
Personalized onboarding flow
Role + team aware. Walks new hire through what they actually need — not a generic checklist.
2
Access & permissions guide
Right sub-group, right approver, real wait time. Shareable completion checklist for manager.
3
Living glossary
Formal terms + colloquialisms. Crowdsourced. Anyone can submit a new term.
4
Team & org context hub
What each ODSP team owns, interactive org chart tied to active work and PR areas.
5
Flights & dogfood guide
Step-by-step setup. Surfaced proactively in Week 1 — not after they notice they're off-flight.
Note on dashboards: Surface curated dashboards — but only after the new hire has team context. A dashboard is meaningless without understanding what the numbers represent.

Avoid context poisoning One monolithic agent carrying all ODSP context will bleed across unrelated queries — giving answers that fit prior conversations, not the current question.
Expert sub-agents per domain Each sub-agent knows its area deeply and stays in its lane. The master agent routes "I'm on multi-tiering, how do I start a Nitro pipeline?" to the right expert.
Prefer routing to humans over hallucinating When no sub-agent has a confident answer, surface the right alias — not a plausible-sounding guess. Mary was explicit: stale or hallucinated answers are an instant turn-off.
